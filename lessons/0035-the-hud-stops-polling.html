The HUD Stops Polling

Lessons 31 & 32 left an honest debt on the books: the HUD checks the player's state every single frame. Today we pay it — with signals.

TODAY

Delete the HUD scene and the game still runs. When that's true, you've truly decoupled the UI from the game — the player no longer knows the HUD exists, and the HUD no longer spies on the player. It just listens.

The debt we're paying

Back in Lesson 11 we learned the doctrine: events beat polling. A wolf shouldn't ask "is the player near?" every frame — a zone should tell it. But the HUD never got that memo. Open hud.gd as it was and you'd find a _process that, sixty times a second, reaches into the player and reads its health, its target, its has_sword… Lessons 31 and 32 even flagged it in writing as "honest debt."

⚠ spying goes both ways

It's worse than one-directional. The player also reached up into the HUD: hud.flash_hurt(), hud.show_banner(...), hud.show_death_screen(), and it even tweened the HUD's health bar directly. The player and the HUD were tangled together — change one, risk the other. Delete the HUD and the player would crash.

The fix: the player announces; the HUD listens

A custom signal is an announcement a node makes without knowing or caring who hears it. The player will shout "my health changed to 12!" into the void. Maybe the HUD is listening. Maybe nothing is. The player doesn't care — and that not-caring is the decoupling.

signal

Declare one at the top of a script: signal health_changed(health, max_health). It's a new kind of event this node can fire.

emit

Fire it when the thing happens: health_changed.emit(health, max_health). Anyone connected runs; if nobody's connected, nothing happens.

connect

Subscribe from the other side: player.health_changed.connect(_on_player_health). Now your function runs every time the player emits.

Part A — the player grows a voice

At the top of player.gd, declare what it can announce:

GDScript · player.gd
signal health_changed(health, max_health)
signal rage_changed(rage, max_rage)
signal hurt
signal died
signal target_changed(target)
signal banner_requested(text)
signal moved
signal attack_started

Then delete the player's HUD references — health_bar, rage_bar, and hud all go. Each place the player used to touch the HUD becomes an emit:

GDScript · player.gd — before → after
# take_damage():  was: health_bar tween + hud.flash_hurt()
health_changed.emit(health, max_health)
hurt.emit()

# add_rage():     was: rage_bar.value = rage
rage_changed.emit(rage, max_rage)

# set_target():   announce the new target
target_changed.emit(target)

# die():          was: hud.show_death_screen()
died.emit()

# no-weapon case: was: hud.show_banner("Find the sword…")
banner_requested.emit("Find the sword — follow the road north!")
◈ the sword pickup, too

sword.gd used to call body.hud.show_banner(...) on pickup. Now the player exposes a tiny announce(text) that just emits banner_requested — and the sword calls body.announce("Rusty Sword equipped!"). The sword talks to the player it already knows, never to the HUD.

The wolf gets a voice too, so the target frame can follow it: signal health_changed(health, max_health) and signal died, emitted in its take_damage() and die().

Part B — the HUD becomes a listener

Now hud.gd's _process is deleted entirely. In its place, the HUD connects to the player once, on startup, and reacts only when something actually changes:

GDScript · hud.gd
func _ready():
    # The player joins its group in _ready; defer so the whole tree exists first.
    _connect_player.call_deferred()

func _connect_player():
    var player = get_tree().get_first_node_in_group("player")
    if player == null:
        return
    # Seed the bars once from current state — a single read, not a per-frame poll.
    health_bar.max_value = player.max_health
    health_bar.value = player.health
    player.health_changed.connect(_on_player_health)
    player.rage_changed.connect(_on_player_rage)
    player.hurt.connect(flash_hurt)
    player.died.connect(show_death_screen)
    player.target_changed.connect(_on_target_changed)
    player.banner_requested.connect(show_banner)
    player.moved.connect(_complete_hint.bind("move"))
    player.attack_started.connect(_complete_hint.bind("attack"))
call_deferred — connect after the tree is built. The player adds itself to the "player" group in its _ready; deferring one idle frame guarantees it's there when we look.
_complete_hint.bind("move") — a Callable with an argument baked in. moved carries no data, but our handler wants to know which hint to dim — bind supplies it.
The target frame connects to the targeted wolf's health_changed / died when the target changes — and disconnects from the old one. The HUD follows whichever wolf you've clicked, by signal.
◇ the low-health heartbeat: a tween, not a poll

The one thing that looked like it needed _process — the red pulse at low health — becomes a looping tween, started when health_changed reports you're below 35% and killed when you heal or die. (It politely pauses for the hit-flash so the two never fight over the same overlay.) No per-frame state-reading survives.

Do it & verify the win

1

Make the changes above. Press F5 — everything should behave exactly as before: bars move, banners toast, the death screen plays, hints dim. Same feel, inverted plumbing.

2

The proof: in the Scene dock, temporarily disable or delete the HUD node, and run. The game keeps working — you can move, target, and fight with no HUD and no errors. That's impossible to do with polling. Undo the deletion when you've seen it.

3

Run the Lesson 34 test again — still green. Refactoring the HUD didn't touch the combat math; tested code stays tested.
